What the platform does, precisely

Three flows carry the practice. Each one is a rule in the schema, not a habit anyone has to remember.

01

Booking & payment

Slots are computed in the lawyer's own timezone, less breaks, exceptions, live appointments and the daily cap, with a two-hour lead time. The slot is held for fifteen minutes while the client pays.

  • Fees settle to the firm's own Paystack subaccount. Docket never holds client money.
  • Clients never write an appointment or a payment; the webhook does, once, idempotently.
  • A settlement reported against any other account is recorded as a flagged failure and returned to the firm for reconciliation.

02

Matters & court updates

One entry — the outcome, the next date and its purpose. Docket composes the client entry and the internal note, closes the day's sitting and opens the next against the court, the judge and the courtroom.

  • A next date falling on a weekend, a public holiday, or a vacation published by that court is refused.
  • Internal notes have no client policy at all — they cannot reach the client.
  • Timestamps are held in UTC and rendered in the reader's own zone. The rule is enforced in the schema today; the form a lawyer posts the update from is in build.

03

Service of process

Service through the platform is pinned to the exact document version and its checksum, with the cause title and suit number snapshotted at the moment of service.

  • The served firm sees the record and that one version — never the matter, the roster or a later version.
  • Platform service requires the other firm's opt-in; an originating process requires counsel's undertaking or an order.
  • Acknowledgement by a practitioner is timestamped and named, and a wrong service is withdrawn at once. The inbox and the acknowledgement are live today; the screen a firm serves from is in build.

What the client reads

The sitting, in language a client can act on

Docket composes the client entry from the same form the lawyer files the internal note in. One entry, two audiences, nothing retyped.

  • Each entry carries the lawyer who posted it and the moment it was posted.
  • The next date and what it is for sit above the thread, not inside it.
  • Court, division and suit number come from the directory, so they read the same way on every matter.

What Docket costs,
and where your client's fee goes

Docket is free while it is in build. No card, no trial clock. Pricing will be published here before any firm is billed. Your client’s fee is a separate matter: a booking initialises a checkout against the firm’s own subaccount, and Paystack settles to the firm.

Step 1

Slot held

Fifteen minutes, and an invoice issued with VAT where the firm is registered to charge it.

Step 2

Checkout

Initialised with the firm's subaccount, the subaccount bearing the charge.

Step 3

Webhook

Signature verified, then re-verified directly with Paystack.

Step 4

Applied once

Idempotent on the provider reference; a repeat changes nothing.

Exception

Settled elsewhere? The payment is recorded as failed, audited, and reported to the firm for reconciliation. It confirms nothing, and the client is never told an unpaid consultation is booked.
